About

I’m Ahmed, a design student at the Faculty of Arts & Design at MSA University in Cairo, studying alongside the University of Bedfordshire programme.

I like work that has to sell something and still tell a story. That might be a shoe poster, a character with a backstory, or a fifty-year-old magazine cover I’m trying to understand.

I switch styles on purpose. A heritage loafer needs warm light and quiet type, a Gen Z sneaker needs noise, and a kid from a broken city needs rust and neon. What stays the same is the thinking: who is this for, what should they feel, and what will they remember.

I write my own copy too. Most of the headlines, manifestos and character backstories on this site started in my notes app before they became layouts.

How a project goes

  1. Dig

    Read the brief, then read around it: the brand’s history, the audience, the old ads, the competitors. The Kawakeb covers started as this kind of digging.

  2. Write it down

    Before any layout, I write the idea as a sentence or a story. Mako had a full backstory before he had a face.

  3. Make the mood

    Pick the light, the type and the colours that match the feeling. Warm wood for heritage, candy blobs for Gen Z, rust and cyan for a scrapyard genius.

  4. Build and polish

    Lay it out, model it or light it, then push it across every format it needs: poster, flyer, packaging, social.
